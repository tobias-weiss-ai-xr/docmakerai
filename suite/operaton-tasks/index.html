Processes and Tasks (Operaton)

Operaton is the workflow engine of the openEduSuite: administrative and collaborative processes run here digitally — approvals, applications, checklists. You meet it when a process assigns you a task.

Your Tasks​

After signing in from the portal, open the Tasklist. It shows the tasks assigned to you, for example under All tasks. Open a task to see its form: details, fields to fill in, and a button to complete the step — sometimes a choice between several paths. What you see exactly depends on the process.

Some lists keep a filter so you see only what matters to you; the number next to a list entry shows how many tasks it currently holds. With no tasks waiting, the list says so plainly ("No task matching filters found.") — then there is simply nothing for you to do.

Starting a Process​

Many processes can also be started by you directly: choose Start process — a panel opens with the processes your university has deployed ("Click on the process to start"). Pick the process, fill in the form that follows, and submit it. The process then routes through the university's defined steps — you will meet it again as a task whenever your part is due.

A process can set real things in motion — user accounts, virtual machines, invoices. Start only what you are responsible for.

If the service stays closed to you, the helpdesk reaches its operators.

Accessibility​

ActionKey
Move through the task list and formsTab
Open the focused task or buttonEnter

  • The task list and the forms are ordinary web elements: Tab moves you through list entries and form fields in order, Enter opens the focused task or triggers the focused button.

  • If you lose your orientation, press Ctrl+Home (German keyboards: Strg+Pos1) to return to the top of the page.

  • High contrast and dark mode: contrast and dark themes come from the operating system or browser — Windows Win+Ctrl+C, macOS Increase contrast, or a browser extension such as Dark Reader. If the app offers its own dark theme, switch it in its settings; the navigation described above still applies.
